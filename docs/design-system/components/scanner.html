Scanner

The camera’s view in the Scan to join dialog: a frame to hold a QR code in, and a line that says what to do or what is happening.

Overview

Scan to join opens the camera so a person can join a Popkin from the QR code on a friend’s phone (QR card). The scanner is the body of that dialog: a dark square that shows what the camera sees, a frame in the middle of it, and one line of words. It only shows; reading the code, and asking for the camera, belong to the dialog that holds it. Typing the code instead is always one button away, in the dialog’s footer.

Anatomy

  1. StageA square, --color-bg-stage, with the large radius. It is dark in both themes.
  2. Camera viewA video that fills the stage and is cropped to it. Decoration: it has no controls and no sound.
  3. Frame62% of the stage, a white edge. What lies outside it is dimmed.
  4. HintOne line at the bottom of the stage: what to do, or what is happening.
  5. SpinnerTakes the frame’s place once a code is found.

Variants

One variant.

Sizes

One size: as wide as the dialog’s body, and square.

Responsive behaviour

  • The scanner is as wide as the dialog’s body and always square; the frame is 62% of it at every width.
  • Under 640px the dialog is a sheet as wide as the screen, from 640px it is centred and at most --layout-dialog-max wide, so the scanner never grows past that.
  • The camera view is cropped to the square, never stretched.

Theming

The stage, the frame, the hint and the spinner are the stage’s colours (--color-bg-stage, --color-fg-on-stage), the same in both themes, because they sit on a camera picture. The dimming outside the frame is --color-bg-scrim-subtle. Only the alert under the scanner follows the theme.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

While it scans the scanner has no role: the dialog’s title and description say what it is for, and the camera view is hidden from assistive technology. Once a code is found the scanner is a status, so “Found it. Opening your invite…” is announced. A code that is not a Popkin’s, and a camera that cannot be used, are each an alert.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabPasses the scanner: nothing in it takes focus. Focus moves between Close and the dialog’s actions.
EscCloses the dialog and stops the camera.

Focus

Nothing in the scanner takes focus.

Labelling

The hint is text, never a picture of text. Scanning needs sight of the camera view, so the dialog always offers “Type the code instead”; no state depends on the camera alone.

Contrast

Hint on the stage
Frame on the stage

Over a camera picture the hint sits in the dimmed part outside the frame.

Motion

The spinner turns as the spinner does everywhere; the words carry the state, never the motion alone.

Touch

The scanner takes no touch. The actions under it are full-width buttons.

Content

  • The hint says what to do with the phone: “Hold the QR code inside the frame”.
  • Busy says what was found and what happens next: “Found it. Opening your invite…”.
  • A wrong code is not the person’s fault: “That’s not a Popkin code”, then the way on, “Keep scanning, or type the code instead.”
  • A refused camera says where to allow it: “Allow the camera for Popkin in your browser’s site settings, or type the code instead.”
