# Popkin videos

One folder per video. The text files are the source; the media is generated from them.

Every journey prompt in [`docs/prompts/`](../prompts/README.md) ends by making one: a short
narrated film of the journey as it now works in the app, with music under the voice. This page is
the standard those films are held to. Where it differs from the defaults of the `creating-videos`
skill (edge-tts, burned-in captions), this page wins.

## What every video is

- **Narrated.** A pleasant, warm voice explains what is on screen. The voice is Kokoro-82M from
  Hugging Face (`onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX`), run locally with `kokoro-onnx`: free, no
  key, downloaded once into `.cache/kokoro`. The default voice is `af_heart`; `"voice"` in
  `narration.json` picks another. No paid or hosted speech service, and no other engine unless an
  ADR says why.
- **Set to soca.** An engaging, exciting soca track plays from the first frame to the last, turned
  down under the voice and up between lines. The track comes from Pixabay Music
  (`https://pixabay.com/music/`, search "soca"). See [Music](#music).
- **No captions by default.** Nothing is burned into the picture. `captions.vtt` is still written
  beside the MP4, so a player can show captions to someone who needs them. `--captions` burns them
  in when a video asks for it.
- **Picture-led.** Screenshots of the running app carry the film. A slide holds a headline of two
  to six words with one word in the accent colour, a number or a label; it never transcribes the
  narration. The frame is always full: a screenshot takes at least 60 % of the width, over a field
  of a Popkin colour or a photograph, never a small picture on an empty background.
- **Moving.** Scenes change with designed transitions, on the beat of the music.
- **True.** It shows and says only what the branch does, in the words of `docs/specs/`. If it
  quotes Popkin's purpose, it quotes `AGENTS.md` word for word.

Format: 1920×1080, 30 fps, H.264 with AAC audio, 60 to 150 seconds, under about 15 MB.

## Folder

```
docs/videos/NN-journey-JJ-topic/
  narration.json     the lines, their scenes and the voice                     (written)
  film.js            the scenes: markup and the functions that move it         (written)
  slides.html        the page that plays the film                              (written)
  shots.ts           which states of the app to open and what to save          (written)
  README.md          what the video shows, its run sheet, what it proves       (written)
  script.md          the transcript                                            (written)
  shots/             the screenshots, PNG at double density                    (generated, committed)
  timing.js          the scene grid and the lines in film time, read by the deck (generated, committed)
  captions.vtt       captions in film time, a sidecar file                     (generated, committed)
  NN-journey-JJ-topic.mp3   voice and ducked music                             (generated, committed)
  NN-journey-JJ-topic.mp4   the film                                           (generated, committed)
```

`NN` is the next free video number; `JJ` is the journey's number in `docs/journeys.md`.
`narration.wav` and anything under `.cache/` are never committed.

## Tools

The scripts live in `tools/` and are added by the first video that needs them, then reused and
improved by every later one. None of them is part of a test run.

| Tool | What it does |
|------|--------------|
| `tools/video-record/` | Opens the app in Chromium and runs a folder's `shots.ts`, saving `shots/`. |
| `tools/video-audio/` | Speaks `narration.json` with Kokoro, fits each line to its scene, mixes the music under it, writes the MP3, `captions.vtt` and the timing the film reads. |
| `tools/video-build/` | Opens `slides.html`, seeks it frame by frame, and encodes the MP4 with ffmpeg. `--check` prints the schedule, `--frame <seconds> <png>` saves one frame, `--captions` burns captions in. |

They need Node with the Playwright copy in `e2e/` (`NODE_PATH=e2e/node_modules`), Python with
`kokoro-onnx` and `numpy`, and ffmpeg with libx264 (`FFMPEG_PATH` overrides). When a tool's
command changes, change the `README.md` at the repository root in the same commit.

## Screenshots

- **From the running app, never from the mocks.** `shots.ts` uses the fixtures and page objects of
  `e2e/`: it imports `test` from `e2e/fixtures/pk-test.ts` and opens states by their key in
  `e2e/fixtures/routes.ts`. Every `/api/**` request is answered from the recording or a stub, as in
  every spec (ADR 0005); no backend and no database runs.
- **It is not a test and not a sweep.** `tools/video-record/` only borrows Playwright to drive
  Chromium. It never sets `PK_SWEEPS` or `PK_BASELINE`, compares nothing and is not run by
  `npm test`, `npm run parity` or CI.
- **Every form factor the journey changes on.** Take each key state at 390 px (XS), 768 px (M),
  1024 px (L) and 1440 px (XL), at double density, in the light theme, and one state in the dark
  theme. A journey whose screens are the same from M upward takes the phone and one desktop size.
- **Close-ups.** Small text is unreadable at 1080p. Save a `detail` shot of the region the line is
  about and enlarge that, instead of zooming a full-page screenshot.
- **Vary the layout.** No two scenes in a row share a layout or a background: headline left and
  screen right, then a full-width desktop, then a phone over the corner of a browser window, then
  a close-up alone. Acts change colour.
- **Frames.** `film.js` draws the device around a screenshot: a phone for XS, a tablet for M, a
  browser window for L and XL. Use the tokens of `docs/design-system/` for every colour and the
  bundled fonts for every word.

## Slides and motion

- **Every pixel is a pure function of film time.** `slides.html` plays live against the MP3 and
  exposes `seek(seconds)`; the builder seeks it frame by frame. Nothing uses CSS animation, CSS
  transition, `setTimeout` or `Math.random()` without a seed, so any frame can be drawn alone and
  the same frame always looks the same.
- **Cut to the music.** Measure the track's tempo and set the film's grid to it. A scene is a whole
  number of bars; cuts, word rises and transitions start on a beat.
- **Transitions are designed, and few.** A change of act gets a transition: a wipe in the Popkin's
  colour, a push, a zoom through a screenshot into the next screen, a phone growing into a desktop
  window. Inside an act, cut hard. One flourish a scene is enough.
- **Show the form factors moving.** At least one scene shows the same screen growing from the
  phone to the desktop, so the tabs become the rail and then the sidebar and the extra panes open.
- **Do not let a frame drift for a whole scene.** A still that slowly zooms doubles the file size.
  Move, then hold.
- **WebGL and WebGPU are in scope.** A shader background, confetti, a device turning in 3D or a
  particle burst on the beat are all fair. Three rules: the time uniform comes from the film clock
  and randomness is seeded; the effect renders in headless Chromium (WebGPU falls back to WebGL 2
  when no adapter is found); and the builder stops with an error if a context cannot be created,
  never encoding blank frames.

## Narration

- About 150 words a minute. Two sentences a scene is plenty; leave a beat of music between lines.
- Second person, warm, concrete. No filler, no marketing tone. It must make sense with eyes closed.
- Suggested shape: a hook in the first six seconds; who the journey is for; the journey on screen
  (most of the film); the form factors; one card for what the tests prove that no screen shows;
  one card for what is left for the owner, if anything; the close, with the logo.

## Music

- **Source.** Pixabay Music, search "soca". Choose an instrumental track with a steady beat, at
  least as long as the film or looping cleanly, and bright enough to be heard quietly.
- **Keep it in the repository.** Save it as `docs/videos/assets/music/<slug>.mp3` and add a row to
  `docs/videos/assets/music/CREDITS.md`: title, artist, the track's Pixabay URL, the date it was
  downloaded and "Pixabay Content License". A later video may reuse a track already there.
- **Mix.** One bar of music alone before the first word. Under the voice the music is turned down
  enough that every word is clear (about 14 to 18 dB below the voice); between lines it comes back
  up. End with three seconds of music alone, faded out. Normalise the mix to about −16 LUFS.
- **If Pixabay cannot be reached,** do not synthesise a substitute and do not use a track of
  unknown licence. Build the film with the voice alone, write "Music: pending" at the top of the
  video's `README.md`, and say so in the pull request and the run's summary.

## Check before committing

- `ffprobe -v error -show_entries format=duration:stream=codec_name,width,height <mp4>` gives
  1920×1080, `h264` and `aac`, and a duration that matches the MP3.
- Save one frame per scene with `--frame` and look at each: nothing clipped, small text readable,
  no blank frame, both themes correct.
- Listen to the first and last ten seconds and to one line in the middle: the voice is clear over
  the music and nothing cuts off.
- Add the video's row to the table at the top of this page.

## Learnings carried over from Jubilee

The sibling repository `jubilee` has 26 films (`docs/videos/` and `tools/video-*` there). For this
standard, twelve frames spread across each of four of them were looked at, and their scene code and
tools were read (the sound was not listened to):
01 "Meet Jubilee", 02 "The Jubilee plan", 05 "Journey 2" and 14 "Journey 17". Where `jubilee` is
checked out beside this repository, read `docs/videos/assets/pitch.js`, `tools/video-build/build.mjs`,
`tools/video-audio/film_audio.py` and `tools/video-record/shots.ts` there before writing Popkin's
tools: they are short and they work.

### What the best two do (01 and 02): copy this

- **One idea a scene, in very large type.** A headline of two to six words fills a third of the
  frame, with one word in the accent colour ("Purpose, **built in.**"). A number is the biggest
  thing on screen ("$29", "April 6, 2027").
- **The frame is always full.** A photograph bleeds to every edge, or a field of the brand colour
  does. No scene is a small picture on an empty background.
- **The interface floats over the picture.** A card or a screen of the product sits tilted and
  enlarged beside the headline, big enough to read, then the next one stacks in.
- **Things build on the beat.** Three panels arrive one by one ("Find", "Message", "Book"); bars of
  a chart grow in turn; steps of a list tick on. The eye always has one new thing.
- **Acts change colour.** Dark photograph, cream, a field of green, dark again. A wipe in the
  accent colour marks the change; inside an act the cuts are hard.
- **A WebGL aurora opens and closes the film** behind the logo. It is the only shader, and it is
  enough.
- **Drawn pictures where no screenshot exists.** Film 02 draws a street and a room as flat SVG in
  the palette instead of leaving a text card.

### What the screenshot film does (05): the model for a journey

- A kicker names the screen and the requirement ("SHOP PAGE · L2-034"), a headline says the point
  in the viewer's words ("“Sold out”, said in words."), and one sentence sits under it.
- The screenshot sits in a drawn browser window. On its own it is too small to read at 1080p; what
  makes the scene work is the **close-up**: the one card or toast the line is about, cut out as its
  own picture and enlarged three or four times, with a ring in the accent colour.
- The phone overlaps the corner of the browser window, so one frame shows both layouts.
- Its weakness: every scene has the same dark background and the same layout, and the window fills
  under half the frame. Popkin's films vary the layout scene by scene and let the screenshot take
  at least 60 % of the width.

### What the screen recording does (14): do not copy this

- A recording of one window at full size is flat: the interface text is tiny, nothing directs the
  eye, and burned-in captions sit on top of the content they describe.
- Its cards are walls of text: four paragraphs with requirement numbers and test file names. Nobody
  reads them in eight seconds. A card in a Popkin film has a headline and at most two short lines,
  and names no file.
- So Popkin's films are built from screenshots in a deck, not from a recording.

### What the tools taught

- **The deck is a pure function of time.** `seek(t)` draws any frame alone; the builder calls it
  once a frame and takes a screenshot. A deck that animates with CSS cannot be rendered this way.
- **Wait before the first frame, and fail loudly.** The runtime waits for every font
  (`document.fonts.load`) and every picture (`img.decode()`), and the builder stops if one is
  missing: otherwise the film is quietly drawn in a fallback face.
- **WebGL in a headless browser** needs Chromium started with `--use-angle=swiftshader
  --enable-unsafe-swiftshader --ignore-gpu-blocklist`, and the context made with
  `preserveDrawingBuffer: true`, or the screenshot is of an empty canvas.
- **Long renders run the browser out of memory.** Reopen the page every 600 frames, retry a frame
  after a crash, and write to `name.partial.mp4`, renaming it only when ffmpeg ends well.
- **Pipe frames to ffmpeg** as JPEG at quality 98 (`image2pipe`), `libx264 -preset slow
  -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart`. No frames on disk.
- **Size is frame rate and quality.** Film 01 at 60 fps and CRF 16 is 58 MB for 65 seconds; film 05
  at 30 fps and CRF 23 is 5.4 MB for 80. Popkin renders at 30 fps, CRF 20 to 23.
- **Fit the voice to the scene, not the scene to the voice.** Each line is spoken at the slowest
  speed that fits its window (1.0 up to 1.8), and the build stops if a line cannot fit. Trim the
  silence at both ends of every spoken line. Cache a line by its text and speed.
- **Ducking is an envelope, not a switch.** The music eases down over about 150 ms before each line
  and back up over about 250 ms after it. Jubilee's quiet piano sat 20 dB under the voice; soca is
  the point of a Popkin film, so it sits higher (see [Music](#music)) and comes right up between
  lines.
- **Screenshots at double density** from a 1280×800 and a 390×780 window, with fonts settled and
  animations disabled; `detail(region)` saves a region with a little air around it for a close-up.
